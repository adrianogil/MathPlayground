Reading the rhythm

A sine wave,
hidden in a star.

A star polygon {n/k} joins n equally spaced points on a circle, skipping ahead k positions each time. The moving dot travels each straight edge at constant speed. Its height is a straight-line blend between consecutive samples of a sine wave.

For {25/12}, the step is just under half a turn. Heights alternate rapidly while their magnitude follows a slow envelope. This is aliasing: discrete samples can disguise the rhythm beneath them, much like a filmed wheel appearing to turn backwards.

yj = sin(2πjk/n)For {25/12}: yj = (−1)j+1 sin(πj/25)

The dashed envelope marks ±|sin(πu/25)| at continuous edge position u; the 25-point trace touches it at vertices. Each row shows two circuits of history, with the present at the left.
